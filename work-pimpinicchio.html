The Concept

Dark '90s, wired for now.

The VOLT Collection is built on a dark-'90s foundation with a cyber charge — geometric, asymmetric tailoring in spliced, architectural cuts and hard lines. Every silhouette, from the garments to the bags, is engineered around the same language of tension and asymmetry.

The signature VOLT print is a pattern generated from the house logo itself — the connecting line that links the first M in Pimpinicchio to the N of New York, repeated into a monogram. The bags are named for the New York architecture that shaped them: 30H, Trinity, Dey.

The Bags — Named for New York

Three signature silhouettes, each drawn from a piece of the city's architecture and engineered from sketch to production.

30H30H Chain Bag
Inspired by The Edge — 30 Hudson Yards, NY

Named for 30 Hudson Yards, home to The Edge — the angled, frameless-glass deck that juts asymmetrically into the sky, suspended over the city. That geometry became the bag: sharp asymmetric planes, matelassé sides, oversized brass rings, and a chain, in bio-based vegan leather.

TrinityTrinity Hobo Bag
Named for Trinity Street, NY

An asymmetric, sculptural hobo — a curved form molded to the body, its soft tension held by structured edges. Named for Trinity Street, it is engineered to fold, drape, and carry weight without ever losing its silhouette.

DeyDey Bag
Named for Dey Street, NY

An asymmetric bag anchored by a macro metal plate — hardware treated as architecture. Named for Dey Street, it pushes the collection's geometric language into one oversized, structural statement.
